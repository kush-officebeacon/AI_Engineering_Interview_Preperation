# Research checkpoint

Date: 24 November 2026. This is the review point from the plan. The documents it summarises are already written. Later sections of the project teach the skills. They do not treat candidate anecdotes as company rules.

## What is solid

- **LinkedIn has a real candidate AI policy** at [careers.linkedin.com/Howwehire/ai-usage](https://careers.linkedin.com/Howwehire/ai-usage). Default: no AI in live interviews or coding assessments unless that round is explicitly included. Preparation is encouraged. You must be able to discuss trade-offs afterwards.
- **Amazon has two official assessment descriptions.** The university page still expects your own code and logs browser usage. The 22 January 2026 news post describes an interactive assistant inside a reimagined technical assessment. HackerRank's Amazon guide splits the challenge into "no GenAI" (single file) and "embedded assistant" (repository). Business Insider's February 2025 article is the source for a disqualification warning in interviews, and it includes a spokesperson comment. These are different stages.
- **Atlassian's public handbook does not describe an AI round.** A Principal Backend PDF from 2024 does: HackerRank, an existing repo, an assistant, 60 minutes, screen share, language expertise not required. That PDF is not a new-grad or India policy.
- **Meta and Canva are the clean Category B opposites of a ban.** Meta: you are expected to use CoderPad's assistant and no other AI. Canva: backend, frontend, and ML candidates are expected to bring Copilot, Cursor, or Claude, and to own the result.
- **Category A is real and separate.** LinkedIn sells AI screening to other employers. Amazon pilots consented interview transcription and uses ML to match roles. Anthropic uses Claude to draft and to transcribe, and says Claude does not make hiring decisions.

## What is not solid

- No official LinkedIn page found that specifies an "AI coding round", a duration, or prompt scoring. 2026 candidate posts exist and contradict each other (one says you must invent the algorithm; one says you may not type).
- Which Amazon OA a new grad in India receives is not stated. The university page says the structure can depend on the country.
- No official Atlassian graduate guide containing the AI-enabled round was found. One intern Reddit post is not a policy.
- Google's code-comprehension pilot is Business Insider reporting, initially select US teams. India is unverified. The full articles were not retrieved.
- Microsoft's live interview-tips page does not contain the AI ban that some trackers quote.
- Uber's AI sentence is quoted by secondary sites. The role guide itself was not opened.
- Shopify: no candidate rule found. Employee memos do not transfer.

## Three questions to check your reading

Answer from the documents, not from memory of blogs. Answers are at the bottom, upside down in the sense that you should try first.

1. Amazon's SDEs use Amazon Q. Does that mean you may use Cursor in an Amazon interview?
2. A Principal Atlassian PDF tells candidates to use a HackerRank AI assistant. Does a new grad in India follow that PDF?
3. LinkedIn's help centre describes an AI interviewer that rates answers from 1 to 5. Is that how LinkedIn hires its own software engineers?

## Answers

1. No. Internal tools are not candidate permission. The January 2026 post authorises an assistant inside a reimagined assessment. The February 2025 press account says interview GenAI is disqualifying unless explicitly permitted. Cursor is not named as allowed anywhere official in this set.
2. No. The PDF is titled Principal Backend Engineer and is marked 2024. The public engineering handbook does not mention the round. Ask the recruiter which setup your interview is.
3. No. That help article is a product hirers can send to applicants. It says the hirer reviews the output and that declining does not automatically disqualify you. LinkedIn's own engineering loop is described on the careers pages, and the candidate AI rule there is "no AI unless this round was declared as an AI format."
